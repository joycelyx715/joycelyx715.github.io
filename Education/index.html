Bachelor’s Thesis Topic

论文题目： 三元氢化物XBeH8（X=La, Ba, Pr, Ac）结构和物性的研究

Title of the Thesis: Structure and Physical Property Investigation of Ternary XBeH8 Hydrides (X = La, Ba, Pr, Ac)


Abstract：

​ In 1908, the successful liquefaction of helium by Dutch physicist Heike Kamerlingh Onnes marked the entry of physics research into the lower temperature of liquid helium (~4.2 K), promoting the study of cryogenics and enhancing the research and understanding of quantum phenomena. In 1911, Kamerlingh Onnes accidentally discovered in his experiments that mercury’s electrical resistance dropped sharply to zero at the temperature of liquid helium, thus setting a precedent for “superconductivity” in human history. Kamerlingh Onnes’ experiments and discoveries sparked a wave of research on superconducting materials in the physics community, giving birth to a series of superconducting theories from macroscopic to microscopic and back to macroscopic, including the “London Equation”, “BCS Theory of Superconductivity”, and “Ginsburg-Landau Theory”.

​ In recent years, physicists’ focus and attention in superconductivity research have gradually shifted to hydrogen-rich superconductors under high pressure. The light atomic mass and high Debye temperature of hydrogen make it an excellent candidate for superconducting materials. Hydrogen exists in gaseous form under normal temperature and pressure, and only under sufficient external pressure can it be liquefied from gaseous to liquid and eventually solidified. During this pressurization process, hydrogen atoms gradually approach each other, leading to the overlap of energy bands and the reduction of band gaps, which causes hydrogen to transition from an insulating state to a metallic state. Theoretical predictions suggest that hydrogen can be metallicized at an environmental pressure of 450 GPa. However, due to experimental technical limitations, solid hydrogen has not yet been successfully synthesized in any laboratory. The concept of “chemical pre-compression” proposed by Ashcroft in 2004 can effectively solve the problem of the difficulty in experimentally achieving solid hydrogen. He pointed out that adding heavy elements M to the hydrogen lattice to form MH4 (M = C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb) can achieve metallization at lower pressures. Since the proposal of this concept, almost all binary hydrides have been theoretically studied in the following 20 years, and some structures have been successfully synthesized experimentally and have been found to have excellent superconductivity. The research on binary hydrides are approaching there end, but an ideal superconductor at room temperature and normal pressure has not yet been found. Ternary hydrides add one more dimension on the basis of binary hydrides, exponentially increasing the possibility of crystal structures. At the same time, ternary hydrides can make up for the disadvantages of binary hydrides, such as poor stability and low Tc caused by a single doping element M. Therefore, the search for ternary hydrides with excellent superconductivity is one of the key focuses of current and future superconducting material research.

​ Based on density functional theory, this paper systematically studied the structures and properties of new ternary hydrides under high pressure (0-500 GPa). This paper reports for the first time the Fm3m-PrBeH8 structure and predicts its relatively high superconducting transition temperature through calculation. The research content mainly includes the crystal configuration, structural stability, bonding properties, electronic structure, and superconducting properties of LaBeH8, BaBeH8, PrBeH8, and AcBeH8 structures under high pressure. The main results are as follows:

(1) Crystal structure and dynamic stability of the four compounds: This paper first determined the crystal configuration of the four compounds and determined the stable pressure range of the structure through the phonon spectra of the structures under high pressure (0-500 GPa): AcBeH8: 50-130 GPa; PrBeH8: 20-500 GPa; BaBeH8: unstable in the range of 0-500 GPa; LaBeH8: 20-140 GPa.
(2) Electronic properties of the four compounds: This paper first calculated the band structure and density of states of the ternary hydrides. The band crossing the Fermi surface indicates that the structure has good metallic properties. At the same time, this paper proposed a method for screening XBeH8 structures with more stable structures by calculating the electron localization function, Bader charge analysis, and Mulliken bond population analysis: the central atom of the Be6-H24 cage structure provides an outward repulsive force, resisting part of the external pressure. Choosing a central atom X with a smaller repulsive force to the Be atom is more conducive to the stability of the structure at low pressure.

(3) Superconducting properties of the four compounds: This paper calculated the electron-phonon coupling properties of the stable structures and discussed the superiority and inferiority of the superconducting performance of the structures using the quality factor. Finally, five data with a quality factor greater than two were obtained: Tc = 125 K for LaBeH8 at 20 GPa, Tc = 146 K for LaBeH8 at 50 GPa, Tc = 124 K for PrBeH8 at 30 GPa, Tc = 134 K for PrBeH8 at 50 GPa, and Tc = 129 K for AcBeH8 at 50 GPa. In addition, without considering the reduction of external pressure, the structure with the highest superconducting transition temperature was PrBeH8 at 400 GPa, with a Tc as high as 193 K.

Key words： First-principles calculation; Ternary hydride; Crystal stability; Superconductivity
